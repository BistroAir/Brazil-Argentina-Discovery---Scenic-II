Rio de Janeiro
Brazil · State of Rio de Janeiro
⚓ Docks R$ Real

To Ipanema: ~5 km · To Santa Teresa: ~3 km

🏛️Activities & Sights
⚠️ Christ the Redeemer: Arrive at opening (7:20 AM) or after 3 PM to avoid peak crowds. Buy tickets online in advance at paineirascorcovado.com.br
Christ the Redeemer (Corcovado)
📷 Photography⏰ Time it
Opens 7:20 AM. Go early for soft light and empty arms. Left side frames Rodrigo de Freitas Lagoon; right side frames Sugarloaf. Take the Corcovado train from Cosme Velho — more scenic than van. November has moderate crowds — not peak season.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Santa Teresa Neighborhood
🚶 Walking📷 Photography
Rio's bohemian hilltop neighborhood — steep cobblestone streets, colonial mansions, art studios, cafés, and sweeping city views. Walk the neighborhood for details the tram misses. Best in morning light. Explore Largo do Guimarães for cafés and local life. Use Uber to/from — don't walk down to Lapa at night.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Selaron Steps (Escadaria Selarón)
📷 Photography
Iconic mosaic staircase by Chilean artist Jorge Selarón — 215 steps covered in colorful tiles from 60+ countries. Best photographed in morning before crowds. Located between Lapa and Santa Teresa — combine with both neighborhoods.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Sugarloaf Mountain (Pão de Açúcar)
📷 Photography
Two-stage cable car to 396m summit. Best at sunset for golden light over Guanabara Bay. Morro da Urca (first stop) has great views and is less crowded than the summit. Book cable car tickets online at bondinho.com.br.
📍 Map
Ipanema & Arpoador
🚶 Walking📷 Photography
Go early morning (before 10 AM) when locals exercise and the beach is calm. Arpoador rock between Ipanema and Copacabana is a famous sunset viewpoint — locals applaud the sun going down. Keep valuables minimal at the beach.
📍 Map
🛒Markets
CADEG — Municipal Market (Benfica)
🏪 Market🐟 Seafood
Rio's wholesale food hall — 100,000m², 714 stores. The real Rio market experience. Saturday 11:30–14:00 is the highlight: all-you-can-eat feijoada at Casa dos Galegos (R$95) with live chorinho band from 12:30. Bacalhau specialist Rei do Bacalhau, fresh fish counter (Peixaria do Zé — grilled robalo R$68), flower hall with orchids at wholesale prices. Zero tourists. Take Uber both ways (R$40 from Copacabana). Open Tue–Sat 6 AM–5 PM, Sun 6 AM–1 PM.
📍 Map
Feira Hippie de Ipanema (Sunday)
🏪 Market
Long-running Sunday market at Praça General Osório — Brazilian crafts, artwork, clothing, and souvenirs with personality. Good for fresh produce vendors on the edges. Sunday mornings.
📍 Map
🍽️Food & Wine
Holly-safe focus: Seafood, fresh fish, açaí, tropical fruits, fresh juices, pão de queijo. Avoid feijoada (pork/beans with garlic). Say "sem alho" (no garlic) when ordering.
Boteco Culture
🐟 Local
Rio's neighborhood bars are social institutions. Order petiscos (shared plates): bolinhos de bacalhau (cod fritters), pastéis, grilled shrimp. Cold chopp (draft beer) or caipirinha. Busy botecos full of locals are always a better bet than tourist-facing restaurants.
Açaí
✓ Holly
Rio's version is sweetened and served cold, topped with banana and granola. Excellent for a beach snack. Widely available at beach kiosks and juice bars.
Wines
🍷 Wine
Brazilian whites from Serra Gaúcha (Rio Grande do Sul) — look for Chardonnay and Riesling Itálico. Vinho Verde (Portuguese) widely available and pairs beautifully with seafood. CADEG has excellent wine stores with knowledgeable staff.

Armação dos Búzios
Brazil · State of Rio de Janeiro

🏖️Activities & Sights
Rua das Pedras & Orla Bardot
🚶 Walking📷 Photography
The cobbled main street starts right at the tender pier — boutiques, bars, restaurants. The Orla Bardot seafront path leads to the famous bronze Brigitte Bardot statue (she put Búzios on the map in 1964). Best in late morning once shops open; livelier toward evening. ~15 min walk to Praia dos Ossos.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Praia João Fernandes — by Taxi Boat
🐟 Snorkel✓ Holly
Sheltered bay with green water and rocks to snorkel around. Beach restaurants right on the sand. Take a taxi boat from the pier (R$20–40/person). Arrive by 10 AM for a good table. Order grilled fish at the beach kiosks.
📍 Map
Praia da Tartaruga — Green Turtles
✓ Holly
Calm bay west of center where green turtles are often spotted feeding close to shore. Morning is best for clearest water. Taxi boat from pier. Kiosks and chairs available.
📍 Map
Schooner Beach-Hopping
2.5-hour wooden schooner loop past a dozen beaches with two or three swim stops. Departs from the waterfront near the pier. ~R$70–100/person. The classic Búzios outing done the local way. Book at the pier on arrival.
📍 Pier
🍽️Food & Wine

La Gare — Mediterranean & French
🐟 Fine Dining✓ Holly
Inside Vila da Santa Hotel. Grilled octopus, fresh catch of the day, signature risottos. One of the most sought-after restaurants in Búzios. Reserve ahead — especially Saturday nights. Rua Vinte e Um, Praia dos Ossos.
📍 Map
74 Restaurant — Casas Brancas Hotel
🐟 Fine Dining✓ Holly
Panoramic view of Orla Bardot. Contemporary cuisine — lobster ravioli, catch of the day, prawns, tiraditos. Reserve at sunset for the best table. Alto do Humaitá, 10 — Orla Bardot.
📍 Map
Bar dos Pescadores
🐟 Seafood
20+ years at Porto do Manguinhos. Moqueca de peixe, pastéis, generous portions, open views of Praia da Armação. Av. José Bento Ribeiro Dantas, 550.
📍 Map
Chez Michou — Crêperie (Cash Only)
Búzios icon since 1986. Savory and sweet crêpes, fresh juices. Affordable. Cash only. Rua das Pedras — right from the tender pier. Avg R$30–60.
📍 Map
Wines

Brazilian whites from Serra Gaúcha. Vinho Verde (Portuguese) pairs perfectly with seafood. Dom Juan restaurant (Rua das Pedras 70) has a well-curated wine selection with contemporary cuisine.

Ilha Grande
Brazil · State of Rio de Janeiro · UNESCO World Heritage
⛵ Tender R$ Real No cars on island
Tender to: Vila do Abraão — the main village
Getting around: On foot, boat, or forest trails — zero cars, zero paved roads
⚠️ Insects: DEET repellent essential — apply before going ashore
Cash: Carry R$200+ — limited card acceptance on island
🌿Activities & Sights
Vila do Abraão — Village Exploration
🚶 Walking📷 Photography
The main village where tenders arrive. Pousadas, restaurants, and small shops fan out from the pier. Relaxed, car-free atmosphere. Walk the waterfront and explore the village lanes — the whole place is photogenic.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Praia de Lopes Mendes
📷 Photography
Brazil's most famous island beach — postcard-perfect white sand, turquoise water. Reached by boat from Abraão (~30 min) or a 3-hour forest trail. Boat is the practical choice for a port day. Hire a local boat at the pier.
📍 Map
Lagoa Azul (Blue Lagoon)
✓ Holly
Calm, crystal-clear snorkeling waters — one of the island's most beautiful spots. Reached by boat tour from Abraão. Often included in island boat circuits. Excellent for wildlife spotting.
📍 Map
Island Boat Circuit
The classic way to see Ilha Grande — hire a local boat for a circuit of beaches and snorkeling spots including Lagoa Azul, Praia do Aventureiro, and Lopes Mendes. Negotiate at the pier. ~R$80–150/person for a shared circuit.
📍 Pier
Atlantic Rainforest Trails
🚶 Walking
Most of the island is protected state park. Short trails from Abraão lead into dense Atlantic Forest — waterfalls, wildlife, dramatic vegetation. The trail to Praia do Abraãozinho is easy and rewarding (30 min each way).
📍 Map
🍽️Food & Wine
Holly-safe: Fresh seafood is the island specialty. Grilled fish, shrimp, moqueca de peixe. Say "sem alho" (no garlic). Tropical fruits widely available. Açaí bowls at village cafés. Carry cash.
Village Restaurants — Vila do Abraão
🐟 Seafood
Seafood restaurants line the waterfront in Abraão. Moqueca (fish stew), grilled fish, shrimp dishes. Casual, open-air settings. Prices are reasonable. Carry cash — card acceptance is limited.
Beach Kiosks
At accessible beaches — fresh coconut water, grilled fish, açaí, cold drinks. Cash only. Bring R$100–150 for a beach day with food and drinks.

Paraty
Brazil · State of Rio de Janeiro · UNESCO World Heritage
⛵ Tender R$ Real Car-free historic center
Tender to: Cais de Paraty — historic center starts at the pier
Getting around: Walk (historic center is tiny), boat (bay islands)
⚠️ Cobblestones: Pé-de-moleque stones are uneven and slippery when wet — wear flat sturdy shoes
Tidal streets: Full-moon king tides flood the historic center by design
🏛️Activities & Sights
Centro Histórico — Four Churches Walk
🚶 Walking📷 Photography
Start early while light is golden on the whitewash and cobbles are quiet. Four churches tell the story of colonial Brazil's caste system: Igreja Matriz de Nossa Senhora dos Remédios (colonial elite), Igreja de Santa Rita (freed mixed-race pardos — most photographed building in Paraty), Nossa Senhora do Rosário e São Benedito (enslaved and free Black brotherhood), Nossa Senhora das Dores (white aristocracy). The whole historic center is car-free and crossable in minutes.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Schooner into the Bay — Saco do Mamanguá
✓ Holly
Wooden saveiro schooners run shared trips into the Baía de Paraty — calm island beaches and green swimming coves. Ask for Saco do Mamanguá, a long fjord-like tropical inlet ringed by Atlantic Forest — the standout natural sight. Book the day before at the pier or through the ship. 2–3 hours. Best midday when sun is high.
📍 Pier
Forte Defensor Perpétuo
📷 Photography
Small hilltop fort above the bay — the view reads the whole colonial plan at once. Best at dusk when the light drops and lanterns come on in the historic center below. Short walk from the center.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Cachaça Tasting
Paraty was one of colonial Brazil's great cachaça producers — the spirit became so prized that "parati" was slang for cachaça across Brazil. Several alambiques (distilleries) near town welcome visitors. Shops in the center pour local labels. Afternoon activity after the boat trip.
🤖 Ask Historian
🍽️Food & Wine
Holly-safe: Caiçara cooking focuses on fresh seafood — grilled fish, shrimp, moqueca de peixe. Say "sem alho" (no garlic). Fresh tropical fruits at market stalls. Cachaça is dairy-free and garlic-free.
Caiçara Cuisine
🐟 Local
The traditional cooking of the coastal Caiçara people — fresh fish, shrimp, moqueca (fish stew), pirão (fish broth thickened with manioc flour). Restaurants in the historic center serve this alongside cachaça-based cocktails.
Historic Center Restaurants
The streets around the Casa da Cultura in the historic center have the best concentration of restaurants. Eat lunch after the morning church walk and before the afternoon boat trip.
📍 Map
🚌Transport
OptionDetails
On footHistoric center is entirely walkable — a few blocks square. Wear flat, sturdy shoes for cobblestones.
Schooner / boatBook at the pier for bay trips. Shared or private. ~R$80–150/person.
⚠️ CobblestonesPé-de-moleque stones are deliberately uneven and slippery when wet. No heels.

🏝️Activities & Sights
⚠️ Borrachudo flies: Apply strong DEET repellent before leaving the ship — these biting flies are the island's most notorious feature. Don't wait until you're ashore.
Vila Centro Histórico
🚶 Walking📷 Photography
Colonial center where tenders arrive — casarões coloniais (colonial mansions), artisan shops, ice cream parlors, restaurants. Relaxed island atmosphere. Walk the waterfront and explore the village lanes.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Praia de Castelhanos — by Boat
📷 Photography
The island's most famous beach — heart-shaped, 3km of fine sand, emerald-green water. Inaccessible by regular car. Boat/flexboat from the village is the practical option for a port day (~R$80–120/person). The arrival by boat with the bay revealing itself is spectacular.
📍 Map
Praia do Curral
✓ Holly
Most structured west-coast beach — bars, restaurants, surf. Calm enough for swimming. Short mototaxi or walk from the village. Good for a relaxed beach lunch.
📍 Map
Atlantic Forest Trails & Waterfalls
🚶 Walking
360+ waterfalls on the island. Short trails from the village lead into dense Atlantic Forest. The island state park covers most of the interior. Wildlife spotting — birds, monkeys, tropical flora.
📍 Map
🍽️Food & Wine
Holly-safe: Fresh seafood — shrimp, fish, moqueca de peixe. Say "sem alho" (no garlic). Açaí bowls, fresh tropical fruits, pastéis at beach kiosks. Avoid churrasco (red meat).
Village Center Restaurants
🐟 Seafood
Seafood and varied dining in the colonial village center. Fresh shrimp, grilled fish, moqueca. Relaxed island dining — open-air settings, casual service.
📍 Map
Beach Kiosks
Snacks, drinks, açaí, fried pastéis on the sand. Cash preferred. Praia do Curral has the best kiosk infrastructure for a port day.

São Francisco do Sul
Brazil · State of Santa Catarina · 3rd oldest city in Brazil · "São Chico"
⚓ Docks R$ Real
Access: Ship docks — walk to historic center
Getting around: Walk (historic center), taxi/Uber (beaches), boat (Vila da Glória)
Founded: 1641 — one of Brazil's oldest cities
November: ~25°C, pleasant spring weather in Santa Catarina
🏛️Activities & Sights
Centro Histórico Walking Tour
🚶 Walking📷 Photography
One of Brazil's best-preserved colonial centers — cobblestone streets, colorful Ibero-Azorean and German colonial architecture. Walk from the Mercado Público to Praia do Mota for the full experience. The Bica D'Água da Carioca (historic water fountain) is a notable stop. Igreja Matriz Nossa Senhora da Graça dates to the 17th century.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Mercado Público Municipal
🏪 Market📷 Photography
Built in 1900, revitalized in 2000. Excellent for photos and gastronomy. The deck offers the most beautiful sunset in São Francisco do Sul — sun disappearing behind the Serra do Mar mountains. Gastronomy options inside.
📍 Map
Morro do Pão de Açúcar Viewpoint
📷 Photography
150m hilltop where French explorers planted a wooden cross 500 years ago. Panoramic view of the entire island, Baía da Babitonga, and the Serra do Mar. The French commander Binot Paulmier de Gonneville landed here in 1504.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Museu Nacional do Mar
One of Brazil's most important maritime heritage museums — vessels from different eras, specialized library on naval history and coastal cultures. Housed in the former Hoepcke navigation company building. Check opening status (was closed for renovation, reopening planned for 2026).
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Baía da Babitonga — Bay Exploration
✓ Holly
Largest navigable bay in Santa Catarina — 24 islands, calm waters. Boat trips from the central pier (trapiche central) to Vila da Glória on the continental side. Dolphins are sometimes spotted in the bay.
📍 Map
🍽️Food & Wine
Holly-safe: Excellent fresh seafood — the local fishing tradition is strong. Grilled fish, shrimp, fresh oysters from the bay. Say "sem alho" (no garlic). Avoid churrasco (red meat).
Pier 65 — Iate Clube do Capri
🐟 Fine Dining✓ Holly
Sensational pastel de camarão (shrimp pastry) and elaborate seafood dishes with views of Baía do Capri. One of the top dining experiences in São Chico.
📍 Map
Calle del Mar
🐟 Seafood
Famous Fish and Chips made with fresh local fish, creative drinks, spectacular view of Praia Grande. Casual and popular with locals.
📍 Map
Vila da Glória — Seafood Restaurants
🐟 Seafood
The continental side of the bay is famous for large shrimp, mussels, and fresh fish served in generous portions. Boat-lotação (water taxi) from the central pier. The Mata Atlântica backdrop is stunning.
📍 Map
Café Doce Verão
Café with view over Baía Babitonga — perfect for a late afternoon coffee and pastry while watching the bay. Relaxed atmosphere.
📍 Map

Punta del Este

The Peninsula — Walking & Beaches
🚶 Walking📷 Photography
The compact peninsula is highly walkable — Playa Mansa (calm bay side) and Playa Brava (Atlantic surf side) are minutes apart. The Rambla coastal promenade connects both sides. November is pre-peak season — pleasant without summer crowds.
📍 Map
La Mano — Sculpture on Playa Brava
📷 Photography
Mario Irarrázabal's iconic sculpture of five fingers emerging from the sand on the Atlantic side. Best photographed at sunrise or late afternoon light. Short walk from the port.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Casa Pueblo — Punta Ballena
📷 Photography
Carlos Páez Vilaró's extraordinary white sculptural building cascading down a cliff over the sea — part home, part museum, part hotel. 20 min by taxi from the port. The sunset from here is legendary. Tanganika Sushi Bar inside has sea views. Open daily.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Gorlero Avenue — Town Center
🚶 Walking
The main commercial avenue of the peninsula — boutiques, cafés, restaurants. November is pre-season so it's relaxed and uncrowded. Good for a morning stroll before the restaurants open for lunch.
📍 Map

Holly-safe: Excellent seafood — fresh fish from the port, grilled prawns, ceviche. Uruguayan white wines (Albariño, Sauvignon Blanc) and rosados. Avoid parrilla (red meat focus). Say "sin ajo" (no garlic).
Boulevard de las Palmeras
🐟 Fine Dining✓ Holly
New 2026 opening by chef Marcelo Betancourt in a restored casona near the port. Fresh fish chosen meters from the restaurant, premium seafood, pastas. Juan Díaz de Solís esq. 2 de Febrero.

Maleza Beach Club — Serena Hotel
🐟 Seafood✓ Holly
Parador-restaurant surrounded by nature at Playa Mansa (Parada 24). Seafood entrées, salads, fresh fish, pastas. Open midday to sunset. Shaded by trees — comfortable even in November heat.
📍 Map
Tanganika Sushi Bar — Casa Pueblo
🐟 Seafood✓ Holly
Sushi and cocktails at Club Hotel Casa Pueblo, Punta Ballena. Open-sea views. Ideal for sunset. Open daily 12–23h. Combine with a visit to the Casa Pueblo building itself.
📍 Map

Montevideo
Uruguay · Capital City · Best quality of life in Latin America
⚓ Docks UYU Peso 22km Rambla
Access: Ship docks at port — Ciudad Vieja is walkable
Getting around: Walk (Ciudad Vieja), taxi/Uber (Pocitos, Punta Carretas, MAM)
November: ~20–23°C, spring — ideal walking weather
Rambla: World's longest continuous coastal promenade — 22km along the Río de la Plata
🏛️Activities & Sights
Ciudad Vieja — Old City Walk
🚶 Walking📷 Photography
One of South America's finest colonial architectural ensembles. Morning and late afternoon are best for photography. Key stops: Plaza Independencia, Palacio Salvo (1928 Art Deco — tallest building in South America until 1935), Teatro Solís (1856, Uruguay's national theatre), Peatonal Sarandí (pedestrian street), Catedral Metropolitana, Puerto de Montevideo. Street art and murals throughout.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Palacio Salvo
📷 Photography
The 1928 Art Deco tower that was the tallest building in South America at completion. Designed by Mario Palanti (who also built the remarkably similar Palacio Barolo in Buenos Aires) — both buildings use Dante's Divine Comedy as a structural metaphor. Guided visits to the exhibition hall available.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
The Rambla — Coastal Promenade
🚶 Walking📷 Photography
22km — the world's longest continuous coastal promenade along the Río de la Plata. Locals walk, cycle, jog, fish, and sit with mate gourds at any time of day. Walk from Ciudad Vieja toward Pocitos for the best stretch. On very clear days, the Buenos Aires skyline is visible across the estuary.
📍 Map
Punta Carretas — Former Prison Mall
📷 Photography
A 1915 prison converted into a shopping mall with the cell structure preserved and visible through the retail spaces — remarkable adaptive reuse. Worth a taxi trip for the architectural curiosity. Café Brasilero (1877) nearby is the oldest café in the city.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
🛒Markets
Mercado del Puerto — Ciudad Vieja
🏪 Market📷 Photography
Victorian iron and glass hall (1868, prefabricated in England) filled with parrilla restaurants — charcoal smoke drifts perpetually through the structure. Buenos Aires
Argentina · Capital City · Final Port
⚓ Docks ARS Peso Spring season
Access: Ship docks at Puerto Madero — walkable to San Telmo, taxi to Palermo/Recoleta
Getting around: Uber, taxi, Subte (metro), walking

🏛️Activities & Sights
San Telmo — Historic Neighborhood
🚶 Walking📷 Photography
Buenos Aires' oldest and most atmospheric neighborhood — cobblestone streets, low historic houses, shady patios, antique shops, classic cafés, tango corners. Walk Defensa street from Plaza Dorrego to Plaza de Mayo. If visiting on Sunday, the Feria de San Telmo fills the streets from 11 AM — choripán stands, antiques, street performers, tango dancers. Closest major neighborhood to the port.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Mercado de San Telmo
🏪 Market📷 Photography
1897 iron and glass market hall — antique stalls, food vendors, parrilla counter inside. The architecture is stunning. Lunch at the parrilla counter inside the market is a classic Buenos Aires experience. Defensa 961.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Recoleta Cemetery
📷 Photography
One of the world's most extraordinary cemeteries — a city of elaborate mausoleums housing Argentina's elite, including Evita Perón. Free entry. Best in morning light. Combine with breakfast at La Biela café on Quintana opposite the cemetery entrance.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
Palermo Soho & Hollywood
🚶 Walking📷 Photography
Buenos Aires' most vibrant neighborhood — tree-lined streets, boutiques, cafés, restaurants, street art. Palermo Soho (around Plaza Serrano) for shopping and cafés; Palermo Hollywood for restaurants and bars. 20 min by Uber from the port. The Dorrego flea market (Saturdays and Sundays) is excellent for antiques and crafts.
📍 Map
Puerto Madero — Waterfront
🚶 Walking📷 Photography
The renovated old port district — converted brick warehouses, the Puente de la Mujer (Woman's Bridge by Santiago Calatrava), the Ecological Reserve. Walkable from the ship. Good for an early morning or evening stroll.
📍 Map 🤖 Ask Historian
🛒Markets
Feria de San Telmo (Sunday)
🏪 Market
Sunday street fair along Defensa from Plaza Dorrego to Plaza de Mayo — antiques, crafts, street food, tango dancers, live music. Starts 11 AM. One of Buenos Aires' great Sunday experiences. Choripán stands at Plaza Dorrego.
📍 Map
Feria de Mataderos (Sunday)
🏪 Market
Authentic gaucho market on the outskirts — folk music, traditional crafts, empanadas, regional foods. A more local experience than San Telmo. 30 min by taxi. Sunday afternoons.
